The '''Special Intensive Revision''' ('''SIR''') is an exercise routinely undertaken by the [[Election Commission of India|Election Commission]] of [[India]] since 1952 to verify and revise [[Electoral roll|electoral rolls]] through house-to-house enumeration, pre-filled forms, and verification of voter data,<ref name="IE why SIR">{{Cite web |date=2025-11-28 |title=Special Intensive Revision (SIR): Why is it important? Why ECI is doing it now and what you need to do |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/india/special-intensive-revision-sir-why-it-matters-and-what-to-do-10390743/ |website=The Indian Express }}</ref> in order to refresh voter records, enrol fresh eligible voters and prevent foreign nationals from voting in the country's national and state [[Elections in India|elections]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on Special Intensive Revision (SIR) 2026 |url=https://ceodelhi.gov.in/PDFFolders/2026/FAQ_SIR2026.pdf |access-date=11 October 2026 |website=Chief Electoral Officer, Delhi |publisher=Election Commission of India}}</ref>

The SIR aims to ensure that electoral rolls across India are accurate, by eliminating the names of deceased, permanently shifted, duplicate & non-citizen voters, while ensuring that eligible citizens are not left out.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2025-09-07 |title=Election Commission may roll out pan-India special electoral roll revision this month |url=https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/election-commission-may-roll-out-pan-india-special-electoral-roll-revision-this-month/articleshow/123742991.cms |work=The Economic Times }}</ref><ref name="IE why SIR" /> The most recent nationwide SIR process was announced on 27 October 2025 by the [[Chief Election Commissioner of India|Chief Election Commissioner]] [[Gyanesh Kumar]] from [[Vigyan Bhawan]], [[New Delhi]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.newindianexpress.com/nation/2025/Oct/26/eci-set-to-announce-schedule-for-nationwide-sir |title=EC to announce nationwide SIR schedule on Monday; first phase to cover Bengal, Kerala, TN |work=[[The New Indian Express]] |date=26 October 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/pan-india-sir-election-commission-presser-today-10-15-states-to-be-covered-in-first-phase/articleshow/124836983.cms |title=Pan-India SIR: ECI to announce first phase dates on Monday; 10-15 states to be covered |work=[[The Times of India]] |date=27 October 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=SIR: India's voter roll revision is worrying migrant workers |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c8jrzgn0nj9o |website=www.bbc.com |date=24 November 2025}}</ref> Intensive revisions of the rolls had been held in various parts of the country since the 1950s, and the 2025–26 exercise is the ninth.<ref name="Tribune robust">{{Cite news |last=Singh |first=Animesh |title=Upcoming SIR to be as robust as first two held in 1950s |work=The Tribune |date=3 November 2025 |url=https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/upcoming-sir-to-be-as-robust-as-first-two-held-in-1950s}}</ref>

The legitimacy of the SIR process was challenged before the [[Supreme Court of India]] in May 2026, which the court upheld, stating that the policy was in consonance with the [[Representation of the People Act, 1951|Representation of People Act]], 1951, and noting that the Election Commission has a constitutional obligation to conduct [[Free and fair election|free and fair elections]].<ref name ="TH-SC-SIR1">{{Cite news |last=Krishnadas Rajagopal |title=Supreme Court upholds SIR exercise as an advancement towards free and fair elections |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/supreme-court-upholds-sir-exercise-as-an-advancement-towards-free-and-fair-elections/article71028139.ece |work=The Hindu |date=2026-05-27 }}</ref><ref name ="TH-SC-SIR2">{{Cite news |title=Bihar SIR verdict Supreme Court hearing, Election Commission highlights |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/bihar-sir-verdict-supreme-court-hearing-election-commission-highlights-wednesday-may-27-2026/article71027586.ece |work=The Hindu |date=2026-05-27 }}</ref>Academic studies have linked the revision of electoral rolls with the removal of obsolete or ineligible entries and improvements in voter-registration systems. <ref>{{cite journal |last=Retnakumar |first=J. |year=2009 |title=Electoral Rolls in India: An Analysis |journal=Journal of South Asian Development |volume=4 |issue=2 |pages=137–160}}</ref> <ref>{{cite journal |last=McMillan |first=Alistair |year=2012 |title=The Election Commission of India and the Administration of Electoral Rolls |journal=Election Law Journal |volume=11 |issue=1 |pages=70–81}}</ref> <ref>{{cite journal |last=Potala |first=Venkata Subbaiah |year=2026 |title=Electoral Integrity and the Accuracy of Electoral Rolls in India |journal=International Journal of Public Sector Performance Management}}</ref>

== Background ==
The Election Commission of India (ECI) periodically conducts an "intensive revision" of electoral rolls. The most recent intensive revisions before 2025 were carried out between 2002 and 2004, varying by state; in Bihar it was conducted in 2003 (see [[#History of intensive revisions|below]]). During 2025–2026, ECI ordered a "special intensive revision" (SIR), starting with the state of [[Bihar]] in July 2025. The meaning of "special" has not been specified. ''The Indian Express'' opined that it indicates the use of ECI's "discretionary powers" under Section 21(3) of the Representation of People Act, 1950.<ref name="IE why Bihar">Ritika Chopra, [https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/the-revision-of-electoral-rolls-10119162/ Why is the Election Commission revising electoral rolls in Bihar, and when have revisions happened previously?], The Indian Express, 11 July 2025.</ref> Former Chief Election Commissioner [[Om Prakash Rawat|O. P. Rawat]] stated that the term "special" is used when exercise does not conform to the standard template of revision, but tinkers with certain elements.<ref name="Shivasundar">Shivasundar, [https://www.thenewsminute.com/opinion/nationwide-sir-is-a-planned-disenfranchisement-of-the-poor-and-minorities Nationwide SIR is a planned disenfranchisement of the poor and minorities], The News Minute, 4 November 2025.</ref>{{efn|It was noted by analysts that there is no mention of any "Special Intensive Revision" in the 1960 Election Rules, under which the ECI normally operates.<ref name="Shivasundar"/> The ECI told the Supreme Court that it had conducted an "SIR" in Bihar in 2003, but one analyst said it could not provide evidence for the claim;<ref name="Shivasundar"/> the 2003 order referred to in its affidavit was reported to be absent from the Commission's website and from web archives.<ref name="ADR Wire"/> For the 2002–04 revision and the dispute over citizenship checks, see [[#Citizenship verification in 2002–03|below]].}} The most important change from the previous intensive revisions to 2025–2026 was the role of the enumerators in clarifying the electors' [[Indian nationality law|citizenship]].<ref name="Darshana Mitra">Darshana Mitra, [https://www.theindiaforum.in/politics/sir-long-road-exile The SIR, A Long Road to Exile?], The India Forum, 1 May 2026.</ref>

Citizenship determination in India is a complicated issue since the [[Indian citizenship law]] is a complex mix of citizenship-by-birth and citizenship-by-descent. Large sections of the populace do not possess documents that can demonstrate their citizenship, especially the poor, the disadvantaged sections and women. An exercise to construct a [[National Register of Citizens for Assam]] took five years to complete, causing inexorable trauma to millions of citizens and eventually left out 1.9 million people from the register, whose status is yet undetermined. The exercise is generally considered a failure.<ref>Kaushik Deka, [https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/cover-story/story/20251006-voter-rolls-who-is-a-citizen-special-intensive-revision-2793070-2025-09-26 Voter rolls: Who is a citizen?], India Today, 6 October 2025. {{ProQuest|3255890550}}</ref>

Since the 1980s, the ECI has been concerned about preventing the inclusion of foreign nationals in electoral rolls. It also received multiple complaints from the chief ministers of border states about the enrolment of foreign nationals. But the ECI had upheld the "sanctity" of the existing rolls, by placing the burden of proof on those objecting to the inclusion rather than the electors themselves.<ref name="IE why Bihar"/> In 1983 the ECI directed that electors already included in the rolls at the last intensive revision should not be deleted merely on allegations of foreign nationality, that deletion required documentary proof produced by the objector from the competent authority, and that the onus lay on the objector, while setting special verification procedures for first-time enrolment in areas with abnormal increases in electors.<ref name="ECI 1983">{{Cite report |title=First Annual Report 1983 |publisher=Election Commission of India |date=April 1984 |pages=9–18 |url=https://onoe.gov.in/reports/First%20Annual%20Report%20(1983).pdf}}</ref>

During the 1997 intensive revision of the electoral rolls in [[Assam]], the Electoral Registration Officers (EROs) were asked to decide on the inclusion or exclusion of the electors. Based on spot checks by Local Verification Officers, the EROs doubted the citizenship of roughly 300,000 people, who were then marked as "[[D-voter]]s". Many of them were referred to Foreigners' Tribunals for deciding their citizenship status, put into detention camps, and even pushed into Bangladesh.<ref name="Darshana Mitra"/>

=== History of intensive revisions ===
The ECI revises electoral rolls in several ways. In an intensive revision, households are verified door to door; in a summary revision, the existing roll is updated after claims and objections are invited, without household verification; a partly intensive and partly summary revision combines the two; and a "special" revision, an urgent remedial measure, can be combined with any of these.<ref name="DH types">{{Cite news |last=Joy |first=Shemin |title=Explained {{!}} Who gets to vote? Why Bihar's poll roll revision is under fire |work=Deccan Herald |date=7 July 2025 |url=https://www.deccanherald.com/elections/bihar/explained-who-gets-to-vote-why-bihars-poll-roll-revision-is-under-fire-3611116}}</ref> The power to order a special revision of a roll derives from Section 21(3) of the [[Representation of the People Act, 1951]], and Rule 25 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 provides that rolls may be revised intensively, summarily, or partly each way as the Commission directs.<ref name="Wire order">{{Cite news |title=In Bihar, Is the Election Commission Revising the Electoral Rolls or Compiling an NRC? |work=The Wire |url=https://m.thewire.in/article/government/bihar-electoral-rolls-revision-eci-nrc-election/amp}}</ref>

According to Election Commission sources, eight such revisions were held between Independence and 2004, making the 2025–26 exercise the ninth.<ref name="Tribune robust"/> The ECI's order for Bihar of 24 June 2025 listed the earlier occasions year by year as 1952–56, 1957, 1961, 1965, 1966, 1983–84, 1987–89, 1992, 1993, 1995, 2002, 2003 and 2004,<ref name="Wire order"/> while a version of the order reported by ''The Tribune'' grouped them into eight periods: 1952–56, 1957, 1961, 1965–66, 1983–84, 1987–89, 1992–95 and 2002–04.<ref name="Tribune robust"/>

==== Citizenship verification in 2002–03 ====
The 2002–03 revision became a point of dispute in the Supreme Court because the ECI treated the 2003 roll in Bihar as evidence of eligibility, including a presumption of citizenship.<ref name="Wire order"/> The ECI's counter-affidavit said that citizenship documents had been sought in 2003, citing four documents against the eleven required in 2025.<ref name="ADR Wire">{{Cite web |last=Dasgupta |first=Sravasti |title=(article reproduced by ADR) |work=The Wire |url=https://www.adrindia.org/node/97205 |website=Association for Democratic Reforms}}</ref> Critics, citing the Commission's own records, argued otherwise: the 2003 press note emphasized public scrutiny of the rolls in local-body meetings and did not mention any requirement of citizenship proof,<ref name="Wire 2003"/> and a former Chief Electoral Officer associated with the revision said enumerators were not told to seek proof of citizenship from existing electors, being mainly to verify age and ordinary residence.<ref name="Wire precedent"/> The [[Association for Democratic Reforms]] submitted that citizenship evidence in 2003 was sought only from a small segment of the population in specified areas, and cited paragraph 75 of the 2003 guidelines, under which no elector already on the roll could be subjected to citizenship verification unless someone objected, in which case the burden of proof lay with the objector.<ref name="ADR Wire"/> The ECI's affidavit argued that, because the 2025 revision prepares rolls afresh, EPIC cards could not substitute for fresh verification, whereas the 2002–03 exercise had used the existing roll as its base.<ref name="Wire timeline">{{Cite news |title=EC Took Double the Time for Voter Roll Revision in 2003 Compared to 2025: SIR Guidelines Show |work=The Wire |url=https://thewire.in/government/ec-took-double-the-time-for-voter-roll-revision-in-2003-compared-to-2025-sir-guidelines-show}}</ref>

== Overview ==
=== Bihar SIR ===
The ECI ordered an SIR in the state of [[Bihar]] on 24 June 2025, four months before the state was scheduled to hold its next Legislative Assembly election.<ref name="Sagar">{{citation |author=Sagar |title=The Bihar Model: Revising electoral rolls could disenfranchise millions and threaten their citizenship |magazine=The Caravan |date=31 July 2025 |url-access=subscription |url=https://caravanmagazine.in/politics/revising-electoral-rolls-disenfranchise-millions-threaten-citizenship}}</ref> Booth Level Officers (BLOs) made house-to-house visits and distributed pre-filled enumeration forms containing names of the electors along with [[Voter ID (India)|Electoral Photo Identity Card]] (EPIC) numbers and other data. The electors were asked to affix a photograph, fill in their date of birth as well as the names and EPIC numbers of their parents. If they were not already included in the "legacy" electoral rolls from around 2003, they also had to attach proof of their citizenship. The ECI stipulated 11 types of documents they could provide. Some of the common identification documents carried by people, such as [[Aadhaar card]]s, EPICs, [[ration card (India)|ration cards]] and [[National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme]] (NREGS) documents, were excluded. Identification documents had to be provided by electors not only for themselves but also their parents as warranted by the [[Indian nationality law|Citizenship Act]] (along with amendments in [[Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 1986|1986]] and [[Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003|2003]]).<ref name="Sagar"/>{{efn|If their parents were born after 1987, the Citizenship Act also requires one or more grandparents to be a citizen. But the ECI spared this requirement. The ECI also did not require any further checks for "legacy" electors from 2003. So the SIR did not involve a full citizenship verification.}}

People had considerable difficulty coming up with the stipulated documents. For example, birth certificates were among the stipulated documents, but were carried by only 2 percent of the population. Matriculation or college certificates were carried by only a 10 percent of the people of age 40–60.<ref name="Sagar"/> It was later reported that the majority of the voters did not have even a single one of the 11 stipulated documents.<ref name="Frontline SIR 2.0">Soni Mishra, [https://frontline.thehindu.com/politics/eci-special-intensive-revision-12-states-political-row/article70220110.ece SIR 2.0: Bihar's shadow spreads], Frontline, 30 October 2025.</ref> However, unofficially, it was learnt that the BLOs had been told to accept Aadhaar cards and EPICs as verifying documents. Due to the tight schedule, the document upload was carried out after the draft roll was published and the ECI reported that documents were received from 98.2% of the electors.<ref name="Sagar"/><ref>[https://www.thehindu.com/elections/bihar-assembly/bihar-sir-documents-of-over-98-electors-received-says-election-commission-of-india/article69971025.ece Bihar SIR: Received documents from 98.2% voters, says Election Commission of India], The Hindu, 24 August 2025.</ref>

When the draft election roll was published in early August, it had 72.4 million names, 6.5 million fewer than the prevailing roll. ECI said that the deletions included 2.2 million dead, 3.6 million that migrated out of the state, and 0.7 million were duplicate entries.<ref name="BBC 11 Aug 2025">Geeta Pandey, [https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3wnx7689l5o New voters list in Indian state includes wrong photos and dead people], BBC News, 11 August 2025.</ref> An analysis by ''[[Hindustan Times]]'' found no discernible pattern to the deletions.<ref>Abhishek Jha, Nishant Ranjan, Roshan Kishore, [https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/bihar-sir-key-takeaways-from-draft-electoral-roll-after-eci-s-revision-101754101995130.html Bihar SIR: Key takeaways from draft electoral roll after ECI’s revision], Hindustan Times, 2 August 2025.</ref>

=== Supreme Court review ===
In early July, petitions were filed in the Supreme Court by the [[Association for Democratic Reforms]] (ADR), the [[People's Union for Civil Liberties]] (PUCL) and several opposition politicians. ADR argued that the onerous requirements for documents violated the universal adult franchise under Article 326 of the Indian Constitution. PUCL argued that ECI was mandated to enable the right to vote for all adult citizens and so it had to ensure that those with "document deficits" were not left out of the process.<ref name="Sagar"/>

In the initial hearing on 10 July, the judges framed the case in terms of three questions: whether the ECI had the right to undertake such an exercise, whether it was following the correct procedure, and whether it had allowed sufficient time for the process. The ECI argued in its counter-affidavit that it was vested with the power to scrutinise the eligibility of voters, which included citizenship. The court contested ECI's claims that the 11 documents were widely available and pressed it to consider the inclusion of Aadhaar cards and EPICs.<ref name="Sagar"/>

The eventual judgment came in May 2026, after the second round of SIR exercises had already been initiated. The court upheld the ECI's right to conduct SIR as part of its constitutional obligation, as well as to verify citizenship status to the "limited extent of determining inclusion or exclusion from the electoral roll". Electors purged from the rolls on suspicion of being non-citizens should be referred for adjudication by a competent authority under the Citizenship Act.<ref name ="Hindu Supreme Court">{{Cite news |last=Krishnadas Rajagopal |title=Supreme Court upholds SIR exercise as an advancement towards free and fair elections |work=The Hindu |date=2026-05-27  |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/supreme-court-upholds-sir-exercise-as-an-advancement-towards-free-and-fair-elections/article71028139.ece}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Supreme Court upholds Special Intensive Revision, backs powers of Election Commission |work=The Week |date=2026-05-27  |url=https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/05/27/supreme-court-upholds-special-intensive-revision-backs-powers-of-election-commission.html}}</ref> The Court also observed that the enquiry of the ECI must itself be quasi-judicial in character, ensure the right to a fair hearing, and the persons should be informed of the grounds on which they are suspected to be foreigners.<ref name="Darshana Mitra"/> The Supreme Court also directed the ECI to include Aadhaar cards among the allowed identification documents, and to publish all the names deleted from electoral rolls in an accessible form.<ref name="SCO Day 5">R. Sai Spandana, [https://www.scobserver.in/reports/revision-of-electoral-rolls-in-bihar-day-5-sc-directs-eci-to-publish-information-of-deleted-names/ Revision of Electoral Rolls in Bihar | Day 5: SC directs ECI to publish details of deleted names], Supreme Court Observer, 14 August 2025.</ref>

=== Second phase ===
The second phase of SIR exercises covering 12 states and union territories were announced on 27 October 2025. They included the four states, [[Kerala]], [[Tamil Nadu]], [[West Bengal]] and [[Puducherry (union territory)|Puducherry]], where Assembly elections were due to be held in 2026. In addition, SIR was also conducted in [[Chhattisgarh]], [[Goa]], [[Gujarat]], [[Madhya Pradesh]], [[Rajasthan]], [[Uttar Pradesh]], [[Andaman and Nicobar Islands]] and [[Lakshadweep]].<ref name="Frontline SIR 2.0"/> [[Assam]], another state due to hold elections in 2026, was left out of the SIR process on the grounds that the Supreme Court-monitored NRC exercise was yet to conclude.<ref name="Frontline SIR 2.0"/>

The ECI modified the procedures based on the experience from Bihar and the issues raised during the Supreme Court hearings. Documents were not collected in the enumeration phase. Instead, the enumeration forms had been modified in order to link the electors with their own records or their parents' records in the previous intensive revision conducted anywhere in the country. Only in cases where such "linkages" could not be made with "legacy" electors would documents be required.<ref name="Frontline SIR 2.0"/> The electors that could not establish such linkages were called "unmapped" electors. In addition, the ECI deployed ERONET software which flagged cases of "logical discrepancies", which involved spelling variations, age/relationship mismatches and other inconsistencies. People mapped with six or more progeny and those with an age gap of less than 15 years or more than 50 years with their parents were flagged. All such cases were asked to appear before the EROs for hearings.<ref name="IE West Bengal SIR">Damini Nath, [https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/how-west-bengal-sir-different-from-other-states-10564447/ How West Bengal SIR played out differently from other states, what it means for rest of country], The Indian Express, 7 March 2026. {{ProQuest|3309272501}}</ref>

In all states except West Bengal, draft rolls were published in December and January and then hearings by EROs were conducted.<ref name="IE West Bengal SIR"/> By April, 64.4 million (6.44 crore) names were removed for the reasons of being absent, shifted, dead and duplicated, and an additional 6.3 million (63 lakh) names removed after objections and adjudication.<ref name="Hindu Phase II">[https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/consolidated-voters-list-of-9-states-and-3-uts-that-underwent-sir-phase-ii-trimmed-by-102/article70853877.ece Consolidated voters’ list of 9 States and 3 UTs that underwent SIR Phase II trimmed by 10.2%], The Hindu, 13 April 2026.</ref>

=== Case of West Bengal ===
In poll-bound West Bengal, the SIR process worked differently as noted by ''[[The Indian Express]]''. The initial process flagged 3.2 million "unmapped" voters, and 13.6 million electors for "logical discrepancies". All such electors had to appear for hearings.<ref name="IT await verdict">Kaushik Deka, [https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/the-big-story/story/20260316-special-intensive-revision-voters-await-verdict-2878637-2026-03-07 Special Intensive Revision: Voters await verdict], India Today, 16 March 2026. {{ProQuest|3311535744}}</ref> In addition, the ECI asked Chief Electoral Officer in West Bengal to appoint "micro-observers" to keep a watch on EROs and Assistant EROs trying to decide the cases. These new officials alleged that the EROs were not deleting ineligible electors and were uploading improper documents. The Supreme Court, which was hearing a batch of petitions challenging the process, noted the "trust deficit" between the state government and the ECI. It then used its powers under Article 142 of Indian constitution to pass a decree in the interest of complete justice, and placed all the remaining cases under judicial review.<ref name="IE West Bengal SIR"/> The Calcutta High Court was asked to deploy serving and former district judges to oversee the pending cases.<ref>Atri Mitra, [https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/west-bengal-sir-judicial-scrutiny-of-voter-list-begins-10547347/ West Bengal SIR: Fate of over 50 lakh voters in lurch as judicial officers start vetting documents], The Indian Express, 24 February 2026. {{ProQuest|3306508873}}</ref>

When the revised electoral roll was published on 28 February, 6.18 million names had been deleted for reasons such as death and migration, and, in addition, another 6 million (60 lakh) names were declared to be "under adjudication". Districts with large Muslim populations such as [[Malda district|Malda]] and [[Murshidabad district|Murshidabad]] were particularly affected.<ref name="IT await verdict"/><ref name="Salahuddin">Ismail Salahuddin, [https://frontline.thehindu.com/politics/west-bengal-voter-roll-deletions-special-intensive-revision/article70703489.ece Erasing the electorate], Frontline, 5 March 2026.</ref> Some 700 judicial officers from West Bengal and the neighbouring states of Odisha and Jharkhand worked continuously to decide the pending cases. In early April, they cleared 3.3 voters, leaving 2.7 million electors as ineligible.<ref name="Hindu West Bengal">Sreeparna Chakrabarty, [https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/sir-controversy-in-west-bengal-electoral-roll-changes-eci-tmc-mamata-banerjee/article70851799.ece What is the SIR controversy in West Bengal? Explained], The Hindu, 13 April 2026. {{ProQuest|3328481300}}</ref> The Supreme Court expressed concern over the deletions but refused to intervene. The deleted voters were allowed to appeal to special appellate tribunals that were set up under the Supreme Court direction. But the Legislative Assembly election now went ahead without them.<ref>[https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/supreme-court-flags-margin-of-error-in-bengal-sir-refuses-to-intervene/articleshow/130231882.cms Supreme Court flags 'margin of error' in Bengal SIR, refuses to intervene], The Times of India, 13 April 2026. {{ProQuest|3329042505}}</ref>

== Special Intensive Revision (SIR) phases ==

=== Phase I ===
The first phase of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) was undertaken as an initial rollout in select States, with [[Bihar]] being the primary example. The exercise was conducted between June and September 2025. According to reports, approximately 47 lakh (4.7 million) electors were removed from the electoral rolls in the State following verification, representing around 5–6% of the total electorate.

=== Phase II ===

The second phase of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) was started on 27 October 2025, covered nine States and three Union Territories, including [[Uttar Pradesh]] and [[West Bengal]], among others. The exercise resulted in a reduction of approximately 10.2% in the total number of electors, bringing the count down from over 50.99 crore to 45.81 crore. In absolute terms, Uttar Pradesh recorded the highest net deletion of 2.04 crore voters, followed by West Bengal with 83.86 lakh. In percentage terms, [[Andaman and Nicobar Islands]] recorded the highest deletion at 16.6%, while [[Lakshadweep]] recorded the lowest deletion at 0.3% (181 voters).<ref name="Hindu Phase II"/>

As per the [[Election Commission of India]], a total of approximately 91 lakh Voters (Not in absolute terms) electors were removed from the electoral rolls in [[West Bengal]] since October 2025, while around 2.04 crore(Not in absolute terms) names were deleted in Uttar Pradesh following the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) process conducted between October 2025 to April 2026.<ref>{{Cite news |work=Mint |title=91 lakh voters deleted in SIR: Hindus hit in numbers, Muslims in proportion — What’s happening in West Bengal? |url=https://www.livemint.com/elections/assembly-elections/91-lakh-voters-deleted-in-sir-hindus-hit-in-numbers-muslims-in-proportion-what-s-happening-in-west-bengal-mamata-11775796829202.html}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=11 April 2026 |title=Over two crore names deleted as final list released in U.P. === Phase III ===
On May 14, 2026, The Election Commission of India has announced the Special Intensive Revision, SIR Phase-III, which will be conducted in a phased manner across sixteen states and three Union Territories.

The states included in this phase are Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Haryana, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Punjab, Sikkim, Tripura, Telangana, and Uttarakhand. The Union Territories covered are the National Capital Territory of Delhi, Chandigarh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu.

CEC Gyanesh Kumar has appealed to all electors to enthusiastically participate in Phase III of Special Intensive Revision (SIR) and fill their Enumeration Forms.
[[File:SIR PHASE 3 STATES and UT.png|thumb|S.I.R Phase III States]]
During SIR Phase-III over 3.94 lakh Booth Level Officers will go house-to-house to 36.73 crore electors assisted by 3.42 lakh Booth Level Agents appointed by political parties during the Enumeration Phase.

With this, the SIR of Electoral Rolls will cover the entire country in Phase-III except Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. After considering the completion of Phase-II of the Census in these three States and UTs and due consideration of the weather in the upper reaches or snow bound areas, SIR schedule for these three State and UTs will be announced later.

This is a significant step in the voter list revision process aimed at ensuring updated and accurate electoral rolls across the country.

SIR is a participative exercise involving all stakeholders including electors, political parties and election officials. ECI has requested all political parties to appoint BLAs for each Polling Booth, in order to ensure full participation of political parties so that SIR is conducted with complete transparency and full participation of political parties.

In the first two phases of the SIR in 13 States and UTs covering nearly 59 crore electors, over 6.3 lakh BLOs and 9.2 lakh BLAs appointed by political parties were involved in various stages of the SIR process.<ref name="Air 14 May 2026">{{Cite web |title=ECI announces SIR Phase-III across 16 states and three UTs |work=News on Air |date=14 May 2026 |url=https://www.newsonair.gov.in/eci-announces-sir-phase-iii-across-16-states-and-three-uts/ }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Chakrabarty |first=Sreeparna  |title=SIR enumeration phase begins in Odisha, Mizoram, Sikkim and Manipur |work=The Hindu |date=2026-05-31 |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/enumeration-phase-of-sir-begins-in-four-states/article71044518.ece}}</ref>

== Reported effects and responses ==
In [[West Bengal]], the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls announced in October 2025 generated controversy and uncertainty among sections of the population, including Hindu refugees from Bangladesh and members of the [[Matua people|Matua]] community. Reports also documented concerns among Bangladeshi nationals who had entered India illegally about the possible consequences of the revision.<ref>{{Cite web |title=West Bengal SIR: Bangladeshi Hindus, Matuas seek answers amid uncertainty |work=[[Hindustan Times]] |date=27 November 2025 |url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/kolkata-news/west-bengal-sir-bangladeshi-hindus-matuas-seek-answers-amid-uncertainty-101764230141177.html}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/bengal-sir-exercise-hundreds-flee-bangladeshis-fear-citizenship-2823268-2025-11-20 |title=Hundreds of Bangladeshis flee Bengal over fears linked to SIR drive |work=[[India Today]] |date=20 November 2025 }}</ref> Concerns among some residents and political opponents centred on the possibility that the SIR could be a precursor to implementing the [[National Register of Citizens|National Register of Citizens (NRC)]].<ref name="IT Bangladeshis flee">{{Cite web |title=Hundreds of Bangladeshis flee Bengal over fears linked to SIR drive |work=[[India Today]] |date=20 November 2025 |url=https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/bengal-sir-exercise-hundreds-flee-bangladeshis-fear-citizenship-2823268-2025-11-20}}</ref>

Several issues were reported following the implementation of SIR in West Bengal. Media reports described an outflow of Bangladeshi nationals from the state, with India Today reporting that 200–300 people were arriving daily at the Hakimpur checkpost near [[Basirhat]] to return to Bangladesh; the report said many had entered India illegally years earlier and had obtained Indian identity documents, including Aadhaar and voter cards, through agents or other intermediaries.<ref>{{cite news |title=Ground report: SIR triggers exodus, Bangladeshi immigrants throng border checkpost in Bengal |work=[[India Today]] |date=20 November 2025 |url=https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/sir-triggers-exodus-illegal-bangladeshi-immigrants-throng-hakimpur-checkpost-2823037-2025-11-20 }}</ref> Other reporting attributed the departures to fears among undocumented Bangladeshi nationals that the SIR's verification process could lead to detection, legal action or deportation, with some associating the exercise with the [[National Register of Citizens|NRC]].<ref name="IT Bangladeshis flee"/> At the same time, the SIR generated anxiety among Hindu refugees from Bangladesh, particularly members of the Matua community, many of whom were concerned about their inability to establish linkage with the 2002 electoral roll despite holding Indian identity and other documents.<ref>{{cite news |title=Hindus from Bangladesh, now Indian citizens, in SIR fear grip |work=[[The Times of India]] |date=4 December 2025 |url=https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/kolkata/hindus-from-bangladesh-now-indian-citizens-in-sir-fear-grip/articleshow/125068153.cms }}</ref> Reports also documented suicides among some Hindu migrants from Bangladesh during the SIR period; political parties and families attributed some of these deaths to anxiety over the revision and possible NRC-related consequences, although such causal claims were disputed.<ref>{{cite news |title=Bengal: Bangladeshi settlers under focus after 3 suicides during SIR |work=[[Hindustan Times]] |date=2 November 2025 |url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/kolkata-news/bengal-bangladeshi-settlers-under-focus-after-3-suicides-during-sir-101762094690327.html }}</ref> BJP leaders sought to reassure Hindu refugees that they could seek citizenship under the [[Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019|Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA)]], while reports indicated that uncertainty over the interaction between the CAA and SIR persisted among sections of the community.<ref>{{cite news |title=Bengal: Bangladeshi settlers under focus after 3 suicides during SIR |work=[[Hindustan Times]] |date=2 November 2025 |url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/kolkata-news/bengal-bangladeshi-settlers-under-focus-after-3-suicides-during-sir-101762094690327.html }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Sinha |first=Bhadra |title=‘I don’t know where to go’—persecuted Hindu migrants stuck in between CAA backlog & SIR scrutiny |work=[[ThePrint]] |date=17 December 2025 |url=https://theprint.in/india/i-dont-know-where-to-go-persecuted-hindu-migrants-stuck-in-between-caa-backlog-sir-scrutiny/2806940/ }}</ref> After investigative reports by journalist Ritika Chopra, published in the Indian Express on 23 September 2026,[1] which alleges internal dissent within the Election Commission of India (ECI) regarding its handling of Special Intensive Revisions (SIR), a process for revision and verification of voter rolls which was already under scrutiny. CJP and other body along with opposition leaders are on protest for removal of Gyanesh Kumar, the Election Commission of India. On October 10th, CJP lead protest [[Jantar Mantar 2.0]] is being scheduled.
