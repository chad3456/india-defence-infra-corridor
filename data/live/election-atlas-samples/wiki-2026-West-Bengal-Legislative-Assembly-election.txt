The '''2026 West Bengal Legislative Assembly election''' was the 18th quinquennial [[State legislative assemblies of India|legislative assembly]] election held in the [[India]]n state of [[West Bengal]] on April 23 and April 29 in two phases, to elect 294 [[Member of the Legislative Assembly (India)|MLAs]] to the [[18th West Bengal Assembly|18th West Bengal Legislative Assembly]]. The votes were counted and the result declared on Monday, 4 May.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/west-bengal-election-result-2026-falta-votes-will-be-counted-only-on-293-seats-tomorrow-heres-why-11441389|title=Votes Are Being Counted Only On 293 Seats In Bengal Today. Here's Why|publisher=NDTV|date=4 May 2026}}</ref> The election saw the defeat of the incumbent [[Trinamool Congress|All India Trinamool Congress]] (AITC), led by [[Mamata Banerjee]]. The [[Bharatiya Janata Party – West Bengal|Bharatiya Janata Party]] (BJP) won the election in a [[landslide victory|landslide]] and formed the government for the first time in the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/bjp-scripts-history-becomes-first-right-wing-party-to-storm-to-power-in-wb/|title=BJP scripts history, becomes first rightwing party to storm to power in West Bengal|publisher=The Tribune|date=5 May 2026|access-date=25 May 2026}}</ref> It is the first time since [[1946 Bengal Legislative Assembly election|1946]] that a [[Right-wing politics|right-wing party]] has won the highest number of seats in the [[West Bengal Legislative Assembly|Bengal Legislative Assembly]].

Over 9 million voters were removed through the [[Special Intensive Revision]] (SIR) prior to the elections. This move has been criticized as erosion of democracy in India.<ref name="r877">{{cite web | last1=Ellis-Petersen | first1=Hannah | last2=Hassan | first2=Aakash | title=Millions in India stripped of vote before critical state election, as government seeks to 'purify' electoral roll | website=The Guardian | date=2026-04-22 | url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/22/india-west-bengal-state-elections-millions-stripped-of-vote | access-date=2026-05-09}}</ref> The election recorded a historic voter turnout of about 94%, surpassing even the [[2011 West Bengal Legislative Assembly election|2011 election]], highest ever for a [[Elections in India|General election for the Parliament or State Legislative Assembly in India]].
In these elections, it was the first time since [[1946 Bengal Legislative Assembly election|1946]] that a [[Right-wing politics|right-wing party]] has won the highest number of seats in the [[West Bengal Legislative Assembly|Bengal Legislative Assembly]]. The [[Indian National Congress]] and the [[Communist Party of India (Marxist)]] returned to the assembly after having no seats since the last election. The [[All India Secular Front]] also retained its sole constituency, while the newly formed [[Aam Janata Unnayan Party]] of Humayun Kabir gained two seats.

The Election Commission deployed over 350,000 security personnel in this state election, and conducted a largely violence-free election in a state with a history of political violence. There were 85,379 polling booths and about 4.5 lakh polling officers involved in the election. New booths were set up in urban areas in high rises and housing complexes due to allegations of voter intimidation in the previous election.

Many political analysts reported strong anti-incumbency against the 15-year rule of the All India Trinamool Congress, widely known as AITC. Several politicians, including [[West Bengal Pradesh Congress Committee|WBPCC]] president Subhankar Sarkar, blamed AITC misrule, filled with corruption, political violence, and especially cases of violence against women, such as the [[2024 Kolkata rape and murder]] at R. G. Medical college, as the main reasons for the defeat of AITC.

Initially, after the results were declared on 4 May, Mathabhanga Naxalbari had the highest winning margin. However, a full repoll for 21 May was ordered in [[Falta Assembly constituency|Falta]] due to allegations of election malpractice.<ref>{{Cite web |title=ইভিএমে বিজেপির প্রতীকের ওপর টেপ! ফলতায় মারাত্মক জালিয়াতির অভিযোগ, কমিশনের দ্বারস্থ পদ্মশিবির |url=https://bengali.indianexpress.com/west-bengal/falta-evm-tampering-allegation-debangshu-panda-diamond-harbour-model-11777384 |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=bengali.indianexpress.com |language=bn}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=9 June 2024 |title=ডায়মন্ড হারবারের কিছু বুথে বিরোধীদের ভোট নামমাত্র! |url=https://www.anandabazar.com/elections/lok-sabha-election-2024/bjp-moves-to-election-commission-demanding-re-poll-in-various-booths-of-diamond-harbour-and-mathurapur-lok-sabha-dgtl/cid/1521153 |work=[[Anandabazar Patrika]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=2 May 2026 |editor-last=Tiwari |editor-first=Vaibhav |title="Subversion Of Democratic Process": Bengal's Falta To See Repolling On May 21 |url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/poll-body-announces-repolling-in-all-285-polling-stations-in-west-bengals-falta-cites-subversion-of-democratic-process-11440564 |work=[[NDTV]]}}</ref> Results for Falta were declared on 24 May, and the BJP won a landslide victory with the highest margin in the state.<ref>{{Cite web |date=24 May 2026 |title=Results |url=https://results.eci.gov.in/ResultAcGenMay2026/candidateswise-S25144.htm |website=[[Election Commission of India]]}}</ref>

== Background ==
The tenure of West Bengal Legislative Assembly was scheduled to end on 7 May 2026.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ghoshal |first=Jayanta |date=7 February 2026 |title=BJP's Advice To Bengal Cadre On Countering Mamata Banerjee's Delhi Play |url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/bjps-advice-to-bengal-cadre-on-countering-mamata-banerjees-delhi-play-10954395 |work=NDTV}}</ref> The [[2021 West Bengal Legislative Assembly election|previous assembly elections]] were held in March to April 2021. After the election, the incumbent [[Trinamool Congress|All India Trinamool Congress]] (AITC) formed the [[Government of West Bengal|state government]] again after winning 215 out of 294 seats in the assembly, with [[Mamata Banerjee]] sworn in as the [[Chief Minister of West Bengal|Chief Minister]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=3 May 2021 |title=West Bengal elections: In final tally, TMC bags 213, BJP 77, ISF and Independent 1 each |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/kolkata/west-bengal-elections-in-final-tally-tmc-bags-213-bjp-77-isf-and-independent-1-each/article34471551.ece |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250915014240/https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/kolkata/west-bengal-elections-in-final-tally-tmc-bags-213-bjp-77-isf-and-independent-1-each/article34471551.ece |archive-date=15 September 2025 |access-date=2 November 2025 |work=The Hindu |language=en-IN |issn=0971-751X}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=3 October 2021 |title=Bhabanipur, Jangipur, Samserganj: TMC adds more to its tally in West Bengal Assembly |url=https://www.timesnownews.com/india/west-bengal/article/bhabanipur-jangipur-samserganj-rampant-tmc-adds-more-to-its-tally-in-west-bengal-assembly/819802 |access-date=2 November 2025 |website=timesnownews.com |language=en|last=Adil|first=Mohammad}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=5 May 2021 |title=Mamata Banerjee sworn-in as West Bengal chief minister for 3rd time, takes oath in Bengali |url=https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/mamata-banerjee-sworn-in-as-bengal-cm-for-3rd-time-takes-oath-in-bengali/articleshow/82400020.cms |access-date=2 November 2025 |work=The Times of India |issn=0971-8257}}</ref>

== Political issues ==
The campaign was shaped by disputes over electoral rolls and citizenship, border security and undocumented migration, and broader debates over identity, governance, women's safety, employment, development, and anti-incumbency after 15 years of AITC rule.<ref name="HinduContinuity">{{cite news |last=Singh |first=Shiv Sahay |date=20 March 2026 |title=West Bengal's two-cornered contest is one between continuity and change |url=https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/west-bengals-two-cornered-contest-is-one-between-continuity-and-change/article70764900.ece |work=The Hindu |language=en-IN |issn=0971-751X}}</ref><ref name="FederalIssues">{{cite web |date=15 March 2026 |title=SIR, infiltration, women's safety: 10 factors set to dominate Bengal polls |url=https://thefederal.com/elections-2026/10-key-issues-west-bengal-assembly-elections-2026-234359 |website=The Federal |language=en}}</ref><ref name="HinduKeyIssues">{{cite news |date=15 March 2026 |title=West Bengal Assembly election 2026: Bengali 'asmita', Matua factor, urban discontent key issues |url=https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/west-bengal-assembly-election-2026-bengali-asmita-matua-factor-urban-discontent-key-issues/article70746371.ece |work=The Hindu |language=en-IN|issn=}}</ref>

=== SIR and electoral rolls ===
The [[Special Intensive Revision]] (SIR) of electoral rolls became one of the central controversies of the campaign. The SIR removed around 9 million voter entries from the rolls in West Bengal, representing about 12% of the electorate. Over six million were categorized as absentee or deceased, while the status of 2.7 million remained pending before tribunals. [[Dalit]] Hindus, especially from the [[Matua Mahasangha|Matua]] community, were affected in certain districts.<ref>{{Cite web |date= 26 April 2026|title= West Bengal elections:PM Modi pledges citizenship for Matua Namasudras under CAA |url= https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/west-Bengal-elections-pm-modi-pledges-citizenship-for-matua-namasudras-under-caa/articleshow/130529950.cms|website= The Times of India}}</ref> The move was criticised as "bloodless political genocide" and erosion of democracy in India.<ref name="r877"/><ref name="g244">{{cite web | last=Sobhan | first=Zafar | title=View from Bangladesh: The hardening of Hindutva in West Bengal will stifle pro-India voices | website=Scroll.in | date=2026-05-08 | url=https://scroll.in/article/1092689/view-from-bangladesh-the-hardening-of-hindutva-in-west-bengal-will-stifle-pro-india-voices | access-date=2026-05-10}}</ref><ref name="z523">{{cite web | last=Rehbar | first=Quratulain | title=India drops 9m voters, or 10% of total, weeks before West Bengal poll | website=Nikkei Asia | date=2026-04-28 | url=https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/india-drops-9m-voters-or-10-of-total-weeks-before-west-bengal-poll | access-date=2026-05-10}}</ref>

In February 2026, the Supreme court told the Calcutta high court to appoint judicial officers for helping West Bengal in carrying out the SIR exercise.<ref>{{cite web |date=20 February 2026 |title= 'No option left': Top court's "Extraordinary" Order In Bengal SIR Case |url= https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/unfortunate-blame-game-supreme-court-tells-high-court-to-appoint-judicial-officers-for-sir-process-11112091 |website= NDTV}}</ref> It was decided to engage 150 judges from district session courts in the exercise.<ref>{{cite web |date= 21 February 2026 |title= West Bengal To Engage 150 judges For SIR Process Following Supreme Court Directive |url= https://newsonair.gov.in/west-bengal-to-engage-150-judges-for-sir-process-following-supreme-court-directive/ |website= All India Radio}}</ref>

The deletion of names from the rolls and the subsequent adjudication process became major political issues during the election period.<ref name="IESIR">{{cite news |date=30 March 2026 |title=Amid steep travel costs, SIR fears, Bengal migrants try to return home for a vote that feels vital |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/amid-steep-travel-costs-sir-fears-bengal-migrants-try-to-return-home-for-a-vote-that-feels-vital-10608576/ |work=The Indian Express}}</ref><ref name="HinduSIR">{{cite news |date=31 March 2026 |title=Election Commission actions under SIR risk disenfranchisement of genuine voters in Bengal: Mamata to CEC |url=https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/election-commission-actions-under-sir-risk-disenfranchisement-of-genuine-voters-in-bengal-mamata-to-cec/article70807014.ece |work=The Hindu}}</ref> The AITC said that the exercise risked disenfranchising genuine voters, while the [[Bharatiya Janata Party]] (BJP) defended it as a revision of bogus entries and illegal migrants. The issue remained under judicial scrutiny during the campaign.<ref name="HinduSIR" /><ref>{{cite news |date=18 April 2026 |title=What does the Supreme Court's latest order mean for Bengal's deleted voters? |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-law/what-does-sc-latest-order-mean-bengal-deleted-voters-10641096/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Nath|first=Damini}}</ref>

=== Border security and migration ===
Border security and undocumented migration were also major issues in the campaign. BJP leaders framed cross-border movement, border fencing and the [[Siliguri Corridor]] as matters of national security and linked them to their wider argument on infiltration.<ref>{{cite news |date=29 March 2026 |title='Bengal last remaining route for infiltration': Amit Shah says Assembly polls crucial for national security |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/bengal-last-remaining-route-for-infiltration-amit-shah-says-assembly-polls-crucial-for-national-security-10607005/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Bose|first=Tanusree}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=18 January 2026 |title=Infiltration changing West Bengal's demography: PM |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/west-bengal/infiltration-changing-west-bengals-demography-pm/article70519322.ece |work=The Hindu|last=Singh|first=Shiv Sahay}}</ref> The AITC rejected these claims as politically motivated and linked them to communal polarisation.<ref name="FederalIssues" /><ref>{{cite news |date=28 March 2026 |title=Amit Shah releases 'charge sheet' against TMC government, says Bengal polls key to nation's security |url=https://www.newindianexpress.com/elections/2026/Mar/28/amit-shah-releases-charge-sheet-against-tmc-government-says-bengal-polls-key-to-nations-security |work=The New Indian Express|last=Maiti|first=Subhendu}}</ref>

=== Identity politics ===
Identity politics remained significant in the campaign. Bengali ''asmita'' (identity), the Matua vote, and questions of language, belonging and representation were prominent themes in several regions of the state.<ref name="HinduKeyIssues" /><ref name="FederalIssues" /> The AITC sought to present itself as the defender of Bengali identity and state autonomy, while the BJP tied identity questions to citizenship, migration and Hindu consolidation in selected constituencies.<ref name="HinduKeyIssues" /><ref>{{cite news |date=25 March 2026 |title=Polarisation still an issue in 2026 Bengal polls, but TMC slightly better placed than BJP |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/bengal-assembly-2026-tmc-bjp-polarisation-minority-vote-share-data-analysis-10600577/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Tiwary|first=Deeptiman}}</ref>

=== Corruption ===
Corruption and governance remained important opposition themes, especially because of the [[2022 West Bengal School Service Commission recruitment scam|school recruitment scam]] and other ongoing investigations by central agencies. These allegations formed a major opposition line of attack on the AITC government's record in administration, public recruitment and institutional credibility.<ref>{{cite web |date=15 April 2022 |title=Explained: What is the West Bengal School Service Commission recruitment scam? |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-west-bengal-school-service-commission-recruitment-scam-7869437/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Chowdhury|first=Santanu}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=27 March 2026 |title='Chargesheet' first, manifesto next: Amit Shah to release document listing failures of TMC government |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/chargesheet-first-manifesto-next-amit-shah-to-release-document-listing-failures-of-tmc-government-10604448/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Bose|first=Tanusree}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=17 March 2026 |title=TMC under pressure as welfare politics meets voter frustration in Bengal |url=https://thefederal.com/elections-2026/tmc-under-pressure-as-welfare-politics-meets-voter-frustration-in-bengal-232281 |website=The Federal |language=en}}</ref> The AITC, by contrast, emphasised welfare delivery, continuity and its development record.<ref name=":0">{{cite news |date=20 March 2026 |title=Mamata woos voters with 10 commitments, says Bengal will be destination for industry |url=https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/mamata-woos-voters-with-10-commitments-says-bengal-will-be-destination-for-industry/article70765593.ece |work=The Hindu|last=Chatterjee|first=Shrabana|last2=Singh|first2=Shiv Sahay}}</ref><ref name="HinduContinuity" />

=== Economy and employment ===

Employment, industrial development and public recruitment were also prominent issues, particularly among younger and urban voters. Concerns about job creation, delayed recruitment examinations, the effects of the recruitment scandal, and competing claims over investment and industrial growth featured prominently in the campaign.<ref>{{cite web |date=18 September 2024 |title=West Bengal economy performs poorly over several decades: EAC-PM paper |url=https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/west-bengal-economy-performs-poorly-over-several-decades-eac-pm-paper/article68654721.ece |work=The Hindu}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=6 October 2023 |title=WBCS Prelims postponed, fresh schedule expected soon |url=https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/wbcs-prelims-postponed-fresh-schedule-expected-soon-check-more-details-here/articleshow/104210072.cms |work=The Times of India}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{cite web |date=17 March 2026 |title=TMC under pressure as welfare politics meets voter frustration in Bengal |url=https://thefederal.com/elections-2026/tmc-under-pressure-as-welfare-politics-meets-voter-frustration-in-bengal-234705 |website=The Federal |language=en|last=Purkayastha|first=Samir K}}</ref> The BJP foregrounded jobs and industrial revival in its criticism of the state government, while the AITC campaigned on welfare schemes and promised continued investment and infrastructure expansion.<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{cite web |date=10 April 2026 |title=Rs 3,000 monthly assistance for women; new AIIMS, IIT for North Bengal: Amit Shah releases BJP Sankalpa Patra for Bengal polls |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/rs-3000-monthly-assistance-for-women-new-aiims-iit-for-north-bengal-amit-shah-releases-bjp-sankalpa-patra-for-bengal-polls-10629715/ |work=The Indian Express|last=Bose|first=Tanusree}}</ref>

==Voting==

===Vote statistics===

According to the [[Election Commission of India]], 70,459,284 voters were eligible to vote in the assembly elections in West Bengal. This includes 36,022,642 male, 34,435,260 female, and 1,382 [[third gender]].<ref name="The Indian Express-2026">{{Cite web |date=28 February 2026 |title=West Bengal Voter List: Over 63 Lakh Names Deleted in Final Roll |url=https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/west-bengal-sir-final-voter-list-names-deleted-10557359/ |access-date=15 March 2026 |website=The Indian Express |language=en |last=Mitra |first=Atri}}</ref>

As per the [[Election Commission of India]], a total of 9,102,577 voters were removed since October 2025 during the entire program of [[Special Intensive Revision]], shrinking total eligible voters by 11.88% to 67,534,952 compared to 76,637,529 in October 2025.<ref>{{Cite news |author1=Shiv Sahay Singh |author2=Shrabana Chatterjee |date=8 April 2026 |title=Electorate shrinks 12% since October, with the fresh exclusion of 27 lakh voters under adjudication |url=https://www.thehindu.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly/west-bengal-sir-voter-deletions-electoral-rolls-after-judicial-scrutiny/article70833724.ece |access-date=8 April 2026 |work=The Hindu |pages=1}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Bhattacharya |first=Aparna |date=8 April 2026 |title=Nearly 89 Lakh Names Removed: Five Takeaways on the Bengal SIR's Brutal Math |url=https://thewire.in/rights/nearly-89-lakh-names-removed-five-takeaways-on-the-bengal-sirs-brutal-math |access-date=8 April 2026 |work=The Wire}}</ref> After addition through additional supplementary lists, the final voter count stood at 68,251,008.

The second and final phase of voting across 142 constituencies concluded on 29 April 2026, with the Election Commission deploying over 350,000 security personnel statewide — including the [[National Investigation Agency]] for the first time in a state election — amid reports of localised violence in Howrah and Hooghly districts.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiatvnews.com/west-bengal/news-west-bengal-phase-2-voting-live-polling-across-142-seats-to-begin-shortly-west-bengal-assembly-election-mamata-banerjee-suvendu-adhikari-2026-04-29-1039257|title=West Bengal Phase 2 voting: 91.36% turnout recorded in second phase|date=29 April 2026|website=India TV News}}</ref> There were 85,379 polling booths and about 4.5 lakh polling officers involved in the election.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-04-04 |title=EC boosts West Bengal poll preparedness with 4,660 new auxiliary booths |url=https://www.thestatesman.com/bengal/ec-boosts-west-bengal-poll-preparedness-with-4660-new-auxiliary-booths-1503577689.html |access-date=2026-05-02 |website=The Statesman |language=en}}</ref>

A huge and historic voter turnout of about 93% was recorded in the state, surpassing the previous record of 84% in 2011 assembly, when the 34 year rule of the Left Front fell to TMC.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jaiswal |first=Arushi |date=2026-04-29 |title=West Bengal records historic turnout, crosses 92 per cent polling in two phases |url=https://www.indiatvnews.com/west-bengal/news-west-bengal-records-historic-turnout-crosses-92-per-cent-polling-in-two-phases-latest-updates-2026-04-29-1039356 |access-date=2026-05-02 |website=India TV News |language=en}}</ref> Over 6.3 crore votes were cast, at least 30 lakh more than the previous assembly and parliamentary elections in 2021 and 2024 respectively, in spite of a decrease of near 11% in voter list.<ref>{{Cite news |date=30 April 2026 |title=What do 34 lakh extra votes mean for Bengal: Shift towards BJP or TMC resilience? |url=https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/what-do-34-lakh-extra-votes-mean-for-bengal-shift-towards-bjp-or-tmc-resilience-13904303.html |access-date=2 May 2026 |work=Moneycontrol}}</ref> Analysts cite the high voter turnout mainly due to the following possibilities:<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bhattashali |first=Amitava |last2=Roy |first2=Prattush |date=2026-04-24 |title=পশ্চিমবঙ্গ বিধানসভা নির্বাচনে রেকর্ড পরিমাণ ভোট পড়ার কারণ কী? |url=https://www.bbc.com/bengali/articles/c208308k400o |access-date=2026-05-02 |website=BBC News বাংলা |language=bn |publication-place=Kolkata and Delhi}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Adhikary |first=Satabdi |date=30 April 2026 |title=ইতিহাসে কোনওদিন এত ভোট পড়েনি...কলকাতায় কোথায় কত ভোট? কারণ আন্দাজ বিশ্লেষকদের |url=https://bengali.news18.com/news/kolkata/west-bengal-assembly-elections-2026-t-nearly-90-kolkata-hits-all-time-high-turnout-sta-2656565.html |work=News18 Bangla}}</ref>
* The conduction of SIR in the state before the election, removed about 60 lakh ASDD (Absent, Shifted, Dead and Duplicate) voter entries leading to reduced total voters and a high turnout.
* Due to high deletion in the voter list, voters have felt importance to cast vote and use their democratic rights. Some, especially Muslims and Bangladeshi refugees, feared if they don't, it may create problems in their future voting rights, further leading to cancellation of other government facilities or even citizenship.
* Media reported high numbers of migrant labours coming from other states for the election. Upon one viral video from Gujarat, TMC accused BJP of breaching Election code by arranging train tickets and foods to migrant voters of Bengal working in other states, who were returning to the state in order to vote.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ghosh |first=Shubhojyoti |date=2026-04-22 |title=পশ্চিমবঙ্গে ভোট: পরিযায়ী শ্রমিকদের ঘরে ফেরার ঢল নিয়ে বিজেপির বিরুদ্ধে অভিযোগ তৃণমূল কংগ্রেসের |url=https://www.bbc.com/bengali/articles/cq8wjl1lnvno |access-date=2026-05-02 |website=BBC News বাংলা |language=bn}}</ref>
* Possibilities of potential anti-incumbency like the previous 2011 election and a general trend all over the world during ruling party change in elections. This turned out to be true after the results.
* In urban centres, mainly Kolkata metropolis, the usual low turnout trend changed. One of the main reason of this change was setting up of new booths in high rises and housing complexes. The residents, especially aged persons, felt excitement, ease and comfort in that while avoiding possibilities of poll violence outside.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Bannerjee |first=Sudeshna |date=1 May 2026 |title=New Town complexes vote at home |url=https://www.telegraphindia.com/elections/west-bengal-assembly-election-2026/new-town-complexes-vote-at-home-prnt/cid/2158543 |work=The Telegraph India}}</ref> In previous elections, there were cases of such high rises and complexes being locked up by mobs linked to the ruling party TMC, during the elections. Such cases done by the ruling party, had been observed since the [[Left Front government in West Bengal|left era]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Gangopadhyay |first=Prabal |date=1 March 2026 |title=তালা পড়া আবাসনে এ বার নিজস্ব বুথ, স্বস্তিতে ভোটারেরা |url=https://www.anandabazar.com/west-bengal/kolkata/central-election-commission-has-decided-to-set-up-booths-in-large-residential-areas-on-election-day-based-on-the-number-of-voters-in-kolkata-prnt/cid/1671129 |access-date=1 May 2026 |work=[[Anandabazar Patrika]]}}</ref> TMC continuously opposed setting up of such booths by ECI.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Bengal Polls 2026: BJP, TMC engage in war of words over high-rise polling booths |url=https://www.deccanherald.com/india/west-bengal/bjp-tmc-engage-in-war-of-words-over-high-rise-polling-booths-in-west-bengal-3898648 |access-date=2026-05-02 |website=Deccan Herald |language=en}}</ref>

===Repoll===
After receiving complaints about obstructing EVM buttons of opposition candidates with tapes, bubble gums or inking the BJP candidates' name and symbol by TMC cadre, the ECI  took steps to verify these claims.<ref>{{Cite web |title=ইভিএমে বিজেপির প্রতীকের ওপর টেপ! ফলতায় মারাত্মক জালিয়াতির অভিযোগ, কমিশনের দ্বারস্থ পদ্মশিবির |url=https://bengali.indianexpress.com/west-bengal/falta-evm-tampering-allegation-debangshu-panda-diamond-harbour-model-11777384 |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=bengali.indianexpress.com |language=bn}}</ref> Such cases were also observed in last [[2024 Indian general election|general election]] in 2024.<ref>{{Cite news |date=9 June 2024 |title=ডায়মন্ড হারবারের কিছু বুথে বিরোধীদের ভোট নামমাত্র! |url=https://www.anandabazar.com/elections/lok-sabha-election-2024/bjp-moves-to-election-commission-demanding-re-poll-in-various-booths-of-diamond-harbour-and-mathurapur-lok-sabha-dgtl/cid/1521153 |work=[[Anandabazar Patrika]]}}</ref> After verifying these cases, the ECI decided to do repoll in 15 booths of two assembly constituencies: 11 in [[Magrahat Paschim Assembly constituency|Magrahat Paschim]] and 4 in [[Diamond Harbour Assembly constituency|Diamond Harbour]] on 2 May 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |last=চক্রবর্তী |first=শুভ্রজিৎ চক্রবর্তী,দেবদীপ |date=2026-05-01 |title=রাজ্যের একাধিক বুথে পুনর্নির্বাচনের ঘোষণা কমিশনের, কোন কোন কেন্দ্রে রিপোল? |url=https://eisamay.com/west-bengal-news/kolkata-news/wb-election-2026-repolling-announced-in-several-booths-ceo-order-magrahat-and-other-sensitive-areas-list/200497512.cms |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=Eisamay Online |language=bn}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Sengupta |first=Buddhadeb |title=দক্ষিণ ২৪ পরগনার ১৫টি বুথে পুনর্নির্বাচন, ফের কবে ভোটাভুটি? |url=https://www.sangbadpratidin.in/west-bengal-assembly-election/news/west-bengal-assembly-election-election-commission-announces-repoll/pid/1185707/ |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=sangbadpratidin |language=bn}}</ref> Beside that, due to heavy breaching in election procedures and EVM tampering, the whole assembly constituency of [[Falta Assembly constituency|Falta]] was scheduled to be repolled.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2 May 2026 |editor-last=Tiwari |editor-first=Vaibhav |title="Subversion Of Democratic Process": Bengal's Falta To See Repolling On May 21 |url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/poll-body-announces-repolling-in-all-285-polling-stations-in-west-bengals-falta-cites-subversion-of-democratic-process-11440564 |work=[[NDTV]]}}</ref>

The alleged EVM tampering by done at the directions of TMC candidate Jahangir Khan, and the entire assembly constituency was scheduled for repolling on 21 May, with counting and results on 24 May.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/poll-body-announces-repolling-in-all-285-polling-stations-in-west-bengals-falta-cites-subversion-of-democratic-process-11440564|title="Subversion Of Democratic Process": Bengal's Falta To See Repolling On May 21|work=NDTV|accessdate=3 May 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.newsonair.gov.in/ec-orders-repolling-across-285-polling-stations-at-falta-assembly-constituency-in-west-bengal/|title=EC orders repolling across 285 polling stations at Falta Assembly constituency in West Bengal|work=News On Air|accessdate=3 May 2026}}</ref>

The BJP won a landslide victory in Falta assembly repoll with a margin exceeding 1 Lakh, highest in the state.<ref>{{Cite web |date=24 May 2026 |title=Results |url=https://results.eci.gov.in/ResultAcGenMay2026/candidateswise-S25144.htm |website=[[Election Commission of India]]}}</ref>
