Legislative Assembly elections were held in [[Elections in Bihar|Bihar]] to elect the [[List of constituencies of the Bihar Legislative Assembly|243 members]] of the [[Bihar Legislative Assembly]]. The votes were counted and the results were declared on 14 November 2025.  

The [[Bharatiya Janata Party]] (BJP)-led [[National Democratic Alliance (India)|National Democratic Alliance]] (NDA) won a [[landslide victory]] securing 202 of the 243 seats in which the election was held, defeating the [[Rashtriya Janata Dal]] (RJD)-led [[Mahagathbandhan (Bihar)|Mahagathbandhan]] (MGB) which secured just 35 seats. Incumbent [[Chief Minister of Bihar|chief minister]] [[Nitish Kumar]] took the oath for a record tenth time. Incumbent [[Deputy Chief Minister of Bihar|deputy chief ministers]] [[Samrat Chaudhary]] and [[Vijay Kumar Sinha]] took the oath as the deputy chief ministers for the second consecutive time. 5 months after he was sworn in, [[Nitish Kumar]] resigned as the chief minister upon his election to the [[Rajya Sabha]] and was succeeded by his deputy [[Samrat Chaudhary]], who became the first [[Bharatiya Janata Party|BJP]] [[Chief Minister of Bihar|chief minister]] of the state. [[Vijay Kumar Chaudhary]] and [[Bijendra Prasad Yadav]] were sworn in as the deputy chief ministers.

For the first time, the BJP became the single largest party in Bihar assembly. The RJD, led by [[Tejashwi Yadav]], fell to third for the first time since [[2010 Bihar Legislative Assembly election|2010]], while Kumar's [[Janata Dal (United)]] recorded its best result since 2010. The [[Indian National Congress]] (INC) fared poorly in the election, while the [[Lok Janshakti Party (Ram Vilas)]] and [[Rashtriya Lok Morcha]] secured seats for the first time.

== Background ==
The tenure of [[Bihar Legislative Assembly]] is scheduled to end on 22 March 2025.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Term of the Houses |url=https://www.eci.gov.in/term-of-the-houses |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250807185225/https://www.eci.gov.in/term-of-the-houses |archive-date=7 August 2025 |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=www.eci.gov.in |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[2020 Bihar Legislative Assembly election|previous assembly elections]] were held in October{{ndash}}November 2020. After the election, the [[National Democratic Alliance]] formed the [[Government of Bihar|state government]], with [[Nitish Kumar]] becoming [[Chief Minister of Bihar|Chief Minister]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=16 November 2020 |title=Nitish Kumar takes oath as Bihar chief minister |url=https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/elections/assembly-elections/bihar/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-bihar-chief-minister/articleshow/79245866.cms |access-date=13 October 2025 |work=The Times of India |issn=0971-8257 |archive-date=7 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007015345/https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/elections/assembly-elections/bihar/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-bihar-chief-minister/articleshow/79245866.cms |url-status=live }}</ref>

On 9 August 2022, the [[Janata Dal (United)|JD(U)]] ended its alliance with the [[Bharatiya Janata Party|BJP]] and [[Nitish Kumar]] resigned as Chief Minister.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Bihar Political Crisis Highlights: Nitish Kumar Resigns As Bihar Chief Minister, Breaks Alliance With BJP |url=https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/live-update-nitish-kumar-to-meet-party-mlas-today-amid-break-up-speculation-3238159 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250429154754/https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/live-update-nitish-kumar-to-meet-party-mlas-today-amid-break-up-speculation-3238159 |archive-date=29 April 2025 |access-date=13 October 2025 |work=www.ndtv.com |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref> On 10 August 2022, JD(U) joined the [[Mahagathbandhan (Bihar)|Mahagathbandhan]] alliance with the [[Rashtriya Janata Dal|RJD]] and the [[Indian National Congress|INC]], with Nitish Kumar again taking oath as Chief Minister of the state.<ref>{{Cite web |date=10 August 2022 |title=Bihar New Government: Nitish Kumar Takes Oath As CM, RJD's Tejashwi Yadav Sworn In As Dy CM |url=https://news.abplive.com/news/india/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-bihar-new-chief-minister-at-8th-time-1547404 |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=ABP Live |language=en |archive-date=22 August 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220822140950/https://news.abplive.com/news/india/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-bihar-new-chief-minister-at-8th-time-1547404 |url-status=live }}</ref>

In January 2024, the JD(U) ended its alliance with Mahagathbandhan and Nitish Kumar resigned as Chief Minister.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Bihar CM Nitish Kumar quits ruling alliance, hands over resignation to Guv |url=https://www.business-standard.com/politics/bihar-cm-nitish-kumar-quits-ruling-alliance-hands-over-resignation-to-guv-124012800129_1.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250213201723/https://www.business-standard.com/politics/bihar-cm-nitish-kumar-quits-ruling-alliance-hands-over-resignation-to-guv-124012800129_1.html |archive-date=13 February 2025 |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=www.business-standard.com |language=en-US |url-status=live }}</ref> He formed the new government with BJP-led [[National  Democratic Alliance|NDA]] and was sworn in as the Chief Minister again.<ref>{{Cite web |date=28 January 2024 |title=Nitish Kumar takes oath as Bihar CM for a record ninth time with BJP support |url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-cm-for-9th-time-twice-in-two-years-101706441309154.html |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=Hindustan Times |language=en |archive-date=4 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240204070844/https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-cm-for-9th-time-twice-in-two-years-101706441309154.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Campaign ==

The campaign centered on a mix of local and socio-economic issues. Unemployment and migration were prominent themes: parties noted that many Bihar youth migrate out of state for work, and competing manifestos promised large-scale job creation.<ref>{{Cite web |date=31 July 2025 |title=Unemployment, Migration, And Jumlas: Will Youth Push Bihar's Mandate Beyond Caste? |url=https://zeenews.india.com/india/unemployment-migration-and-jumlas-will-youth-push-bihar-s-mandate-beyond-caste-2939430.html |access-date=27 October 2025 |website=Zee News |language=en}}</ref> Caste politics also remained a key factor. All major parties pledged to support a new caste census and "social justice" measures, reflecting widespread calls to address the state's caste-based inequalities.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Bhaumik |first=Aaratrika |date=14 August 2023 |title=Explained: Why is Bihar's caste-based survey facing a challenge in the Supreme Court? |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/explained-why-is-bihars-caste-based-survey-facing-a-challenge-in-the-supreme-court/article67167395.ece |access-date=27 October 2025 |work=The Hindu |language=en-IN |issn=0971-751X |archive-date=2 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251102141329/https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/explained-why-is-bihars-caste-based-survey-facing-a-challenge-in-the-supreme-court/article67167395.ece |url-status=live }}</ref> Corruption and governance were attacked by the opposition; for example, RJD leader Tejashwi Yadav accused the Nitish Kumar government of "institutionalising corruption" and misusing government schemes (like a women's outreach programme) for electioneering.<ref>{{cite web |title=Nitish institutionalised corruption in Bihar, claims Tejashwi; JD(U) dubs allegations baseless |website=The Tribune |date=19 April 2025 |url=https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/nitish-institutionalised-corruption-in-bihar-claims-tejashwi-jdu-dubs-allegations-baseless/ |access-date=7 October 2025 |archive-date=19 May 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250519211352/https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/nitish-institutionalised-corruption-in-bihar-claims-tejashwi-jdu-dubs-allegations-baseless/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The ruling alliance countered by criticizing the RJD's past ("jungle raj") and highlighting its own welfare record. A major flashpoint was the voter roll revision (Special Intensive Revision, SIR) carried out by the Election Commission. Opposition parties claimed the intensive revision was a partisan tool, alleging mass deletions of voters and promising protests or even an election boycott.<ref>{{cite news |last=Kumar |first=Supriya |title=India's revision of voter rolls could disenfranchise millions, critics warn |newspaper=The Washington Post |date=31 July 2025 |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/07/31/india-voter-rolls-revision-bihar-bjp/ |access-date=7 October 2025}}</ref>

=== Party campaign strategies ===

* '''[[National Democratic Alliance|NDA]] (BJP, JD(U), LJP(R), RLM, HAM(S))''': The ruling coalition ran on a platform of development and social welfare. It emphasized caste-based outreach and welfare delivery, and frequently invoked Prime Minister Modi's leadership. The BJP also attacked the RJD over law and order and historic scams, while JD(U) emphasized Nitish Kumar's governance. Seat-sharing talks between BJP and JD(U) indicated a near-equal division.
* '''[[Rashtriya Janata Dal|RJD]] (leading [[Mahagathbandhan (Bihar)|Mahagathbandhan]])''': The RJD centered its campaign on employment, youth issues, and anti-incumbency. Tejashwi Yadav pledged massive job creation and ran a digital-heavy campaign, including AI-generated videos and memes. The RJD organized backward caste meetings and positioned itself as the party of Dalits, OBCs, and the poor.<ref>{{cite web |title=Five key reasons why Bihar election 2025 is high-stakes contest — shifting loyalties, popularity swings and Op Sindoor |date=29 May 2025 |url=https://www.livemint.com/politics/bihar-election-is-high-stakes-contest-shifting-loyalties-popularity-op-sindoor-nitish-kumar-tejashwi-prashant-kishor-11748510162672.html |access-date=31 July 2025 |archive-date=25 July 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250725055345/https://www.livemint.com/politics/bihar-election-is-high-stakes-contest-shifting-loyalties-popularity-op-sindoor-nitish-kumar-tejashwi-prashant-kishor-11748510162672.html |url-status=live }}</ref>
* '''[[Jan Suraaj Party]]''': Led by [[Prashant Kishor]], Jan Suraaj positioned itself as an alternative to both NDA and the INDIA bloc. == Controversies ==

=== Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls ===
{{See also|Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India}}
On 24 June 2025, the Election Commission (EC) notified that it will conduct a Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls in Bihar before the elections. The exercise requires all the voters from the state to fill forms to be included in the voter list. People whose names were not in the 2003 voter lists need to provide additional documents. The notification also mentioned that the documents needed to be submitted within a month (with 25 July being the deadline).

Further, the voters need to provide one of the eleven documents mandated by the EC, with the common documents such as the [[Aadhaar]] card, voter ID card and ration cards, not included as valid documents. A significant number of people in Bihar do not have any of the 11 documents. Furthermore, a significant population of the state migrate in other parts of India for work or study and it is estimated that at least 75 lakh (7.5 million) people from Bihar migrate to other parts of India.<ref name="Nizam 2022">{{cite journal |last1=Nizam |first1=Arif |last2=Sivakumar |first2=P. |last3=Rajan |first3=S. Such factors have led to fears of mass exclusion of voters. Opposition parties – such as the [[Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance|INDIA alliance]] – alleged that such an exercise will favor the ruling [[National Democratic Alliance|NDA alliance]]. The Election Commission denied these allegations and claimed that the exercise is lawful and constitutional.<ref name="NIE 2025">{{cite web |title=Inside the SIR of electoral rolls in Bihar: Process, concerns and political fallout |website=The New Indian Express |date=9 July 2025 |url=https://www.newindianexpress.com/explainers/2025/Jul/09/inside-the-sir-of-electoral-rolls-in-bihar-process-concerns-and-political-fallout |access-date=7 October 2025 |archive-date=6 September 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250906155146/https://www.newindianexpress.com/explainers/2025/Jul/09/inside-the-sir-of-electoral-rolls-in-bihar-process-concerns-and-political-fallout |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Korada |first1=Pavan |title=EC's Directive to Update Voter Lists in Bihar Risks Mass Exclusion.
